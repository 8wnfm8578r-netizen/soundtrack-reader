The Lantern Road

I. The valley

At the first light of morning, the road descended into a valley that seemed to have forgotten the rest of the world. Mist lay in the hollows, and the roofs of distant houses emerged from it like islands in a quiet sea.

Elian stopped beside a low stone wall. For the first time in weeks, there was nothing demanding his attention. The horse lowered its head to the grass, and a little stream made its way between the stones.

He had carried the letter for seven days. Its seal was intact, its edges softened by rain and the warmth of his coat. He wondered whether the person waiting for it would recognize the handwriting.

Across the valley, a bell sounded. Another answered from somewhere beyond the ridge. He listened until the last note disappeared, then took up the reins and continued down the road.

By noon the mist had lifted. The fields were green, the sky was pale, and the road ahead appeared entirely ordinary.

II. The empty gate

The gate stood open. That was the first thing he noticed, and for several moments it was the only thing he could think about.

No guard called from the tower. No cart waited beneath the arch. A strip of blue cloth hung from a nail in the wall, moving gently in a wind that carried the smell of wet ash.

Elian dismounted. The sound of his boots on the cobbles seemed impossibly loud. He passed a shuttered shop, then a fountain whose basin had been emptied of water.

Something moved in the passage ahead. He reached for the lantern at his belt, though there was still daylight enough to see the stones beneath his feet.

At the end of the passage stood a door he remembered from childhood. Someone had drawn a circle around its handle in white chalk. From the other side came three deliberate knocks.

III. The signal

He opened the door and found the stairs descending into darkness. At their foot, a single lantern burned beside a table covered in maps.

The woman waiting there looked up. She was older than he remembered, but her voice had not changed. “You came,” she said.

He placed the letter on the table. She broke the seal, read the first line, and closed her eyes. Then she took a small brass key from her pocket and handed it to him.

Above them, a bell began to ring. This time it did not stop. Other bells joined it, one after another, until the whole town seemed to vibrate with their sound.

Elian climbed the stairs. Beyond the rooftops, a line of lights appeared along the ridge. He understood at last why the road had been left open, and why he had been sent to find it.
